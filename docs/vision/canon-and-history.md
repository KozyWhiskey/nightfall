# Canon and World History

**Status:** Locked  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-17

## The Solar Concord

Before Nightfall, Vesper lived beneath two suns. **Solas**, the Great Sun, gave warmth, seasons, and physical life. **Umbra**, the Shadow Sun, cast the subtler light that fed the **Aetherial Weave**: the raw substrate of magic.

The twin light created the **Solar Concord**, an age of confidence, art, and practical magic. Cities used the Weave as infrastructure. Mages were builders, healers, engineers, and astronomers; their certainty in the permanence of the suns was their greatest blind spot.

## The Nightfall

Nightfall was not a collision between the suns. It began with a dark fissure in Umbra. Over three days the cracks spread; on the fourth, Umbra **shattered**.

Umbra's fragments fell as anti-reality, negating physical law where they struck. The largest void shard struck Solas. Solas did not explode: it was extinguished, and Vesper entered its enduring twilight.

This is the canonical origin of the setting. Do not replace it with a generic war, demon invasion, or sun collision.

## The Gloom

The Gloom is the lingering death-scream of a magical sun: a metaphysical contaminant with a semi-sentient, entropic will. It is not a conventional villain. It breaks down complex systems—life, memory, thought, craft, and hope—toward silence and uniform decay.

- Low Gloom feels like a chill, heavy silence, and the sense of being watched.
- High Gloom becomes oily mist, warped space, stolen voices, and hallucinations at the edge of sight.
- It is drawn to fear, grief, despair, and abandonment, but player-facing rules must remain readable rather than punitive or opaque.

The game represents this through the Gloom meter and light Strain, not a separate heavy sanity/Resolve subsystem. Events, enemies, and locations should express the hallucination and memory-decay flavor.

## Light infrastructure: Way-lanterns and Haven pillars

Pre-Nightfall civilization built massive **Way-lanterns** that project aetherial fields capable of holding the Gloom at bay. They are rare, ancient anchors around which surviving settlements may form.

Player Havens use a distinct, smaller **ring of ten pillar-lights** as their defense/HP system. A pillar snuffed by a wipe is a visible loss of the Haven's protection; at zero, the Haven falls.

| System | Role |
|--------|------|
| Way-lantern | Rare ancient infrastructure; settlement/waypoint anchor and exploration lore |
| Haven pillar ring | Player Haven HP/defense; ten lights, wipe loss, Ember-Shard restoration |

## Havenwood and the Whisperwood

**Havenwood** is a canonical example of a settlement founded around four Way-lanterns arranged in a diamond: forge/garrison to the north, sanctuary to the east, archive to the south, and a fortified Gloom-Gate to the west.

The player still names their own Haven. Havenwood is a world-history reference, potential discovered settlement, and tonal model—not a forced name for every campaign.

**Whisperwood** is the canonical Band-1 forest ecology: gnarled black bark, sickly fungi, shifting paths, stolen voices, and Gloomfang Hounds. It is an ideal initial frontier template, but seeded regional names can preserve procedural variety.

## Exploration law

Every important ruined settlement or waypoint should reveal both a loss and a possibility:

1. What did it build with Umbra's light?
2. How did Nightfall and the Gloom break it?
3. What can the player preserve: people, a school, a blueprint, an affix family, or a fragment of protection?

This law connects world lore directly to permanent discovery rewards.
